$FTEC and $VGT are close to the same fund. Both own U.S. tech stocks from the same MSCI 25/50 index family, and their top 10 holdings were identical as of September 30, 2026. $FTEC is a little cheaper (0.084% vs 0.09%) and slightly ahead over 1, 3 and 5 years. $VGT has the longer record, the slightly better 10 year return (24.72% vs 24.50% a year at NAV) and about seven times the assets, per Fidelity and Vanguard.

People search "$FTEC AUM", "$FTEC holdings" and "$VGT holdings full list" on this site every week, so let's settle it properly. I'm a dividend investor first, but I've always thought every income investor should understand the growth side of the market too. Tech is where most of the market's growth has come from, and these two funds are the cheapest pure way to own it.

Why they're almost the same fund

Both funds follow an MSCI index of U.S. information technology stocks that includes large, mid and small caps (that's what "IMI" means). Both use a "25/50" cap, which limits how much any one company can weigh and how much the biggest names can add up to together. That's why you won't see either fund put 25% in one stock.

This is the part most people miss: the cap is also why $AVGO is only about 4% here even though it's one of the biggest tech companies in the world. The rule trims the largest names so the fund stays diversified enough to meet regulatory limits.

The result is that $FTEC and $VGT own the same companies in nearly the same weights. $VGT holds a few more names (318 vs 295), but those extra stocks are tiny and barely move the needle.

What I take from this:

  • The gap is tiny. The two funds are within about a third of a percent a year in every period. That's noise, not a real edge.
  • In dollars, it's close to a wash. $10,000 at 24.72% a year for 10 years grows to about $91,100 in $VGT. At 24.50% in $FTEC it's about $89,500. Over 5 years, $FTEC comes out slightly ahead, about $26,100 versus $25,900.
  • Both have been monster performers. Roughly 9x your money in 10 years. That's also a warning: nothing grows like that forever, and the same concentration that drove it can hurt on the way down.

$FTEC AUM vs $VGT AUM: does size matter?

$VGT's ETF shares held about $154.5 billion at the end of September, per Vanguard. $FTEC held about $22.1 billion, per Fidelity. Both are huge, and both trade with tight spreads for regular investors.

Size matters most if you trade big blocks or use options, where $VGT's bigger market can help. For a long term buy and hold investor adding money every month, I don't think the AUM gap should decide anything.

The $VGT split and share price

Vanguard split $VGT 8 for 1, effective April 21, 2026. That's why the share price dropped from the hundreds to around $119 (market price on October 23, 2026, per Vanguard). The split didn't change the value of anyone's holdings or the fund's returns. It just made each share cheaper, which is nice if your broker doesn't do fractional shares.

Does $FTEC or $VGT pay a dividend?

Both pay small dividends, and the 30 day SEC yields were 0.38% for $FTEC and 0.33% for $VGT as of September 30, 2026. On $10,000 that's around $35 a year either way. These are growth funds, full stop. If you need income from your portfolio, it has to come from somewhere else, like the funds ranked on TopDividendETFs.com.

Personally, I like the barbell approach for a lot of investors: let a low cost growth fund do the compounding, and let dividend funds pay the bills. Just don't confuse the two jobs.

$FTEC vs $VGT vs $QQQ and other growth ETFs

People often compare these with $QQQ. $QQQ isn't a tech sector fund. It holds the Nasdaq 100, so it also owns names like Amazon, Meta, Alphabet, Tesla and Costco, which aren't classified as information technology. $FTEC and $VGT are pure sector bets.

If you want growth without going all in on one sector, a broad growth index fund spreads you across more of the market. My $VUG vs $VONG vs $MGK vs $VOOG comparison covers Vanguard's options, and what is $IWY breaks down the iShares mega cap growth fund.

The risks

Single sector risk. You own one sector. If tech has a bad few years, there's nothing in these funds to offset it.

Concentration. About 45% in three stocks and 63.7% in the top 10. A rough year for $NVDA or $AAPL shows up immediately.

Valuation. $VGT's P/E ratio was 32.8x at the end of September, per Vanguard. You're paying up for expected growth, and if it disappoints, prices can fall fast.

Recency bias. A 24% a year decade makes it easy to assume the next one looks the same. It might not. Tech funds can drop 30% or more in a bad year.

Who should pick which

  • Pick $FTEC if you invest at Fidelity, want the lowest fee and don't care about fund size.
  • Pick $VGT if you invest at Vanguard, want the longest track record, or trade options or large blocks where liquidity matters.
  • Don't own both. They hold the same stocks. Owning both just doubles your paperwork.
  • Skip both if you already own a big chunk of $QQQ, $VUG or $MGK. You'd mostly be buying the same mega cap tech again.

My takeaway

$FTEC vs $VGT is one of those debates where both answers are right. They track nearly the same index, hold the same top 10 and have returned almost exactly the same over every period. $FTEC wins on fee by a hair. $VGT wins on size and history. Pick the one that's easiest at your brokerage, set it up, and spend your energy on the bigger decision: how much of your portfolio should be in one sector at all.
