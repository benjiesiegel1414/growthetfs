$VEU is the Vanguard FTSE All-World ex-US ETF, an index fund that owns almost every large and mid size company in the world outside the United States: about 3,800 stocks across developed and emerging markets, led by Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung and ASML. It launched March 2, 2007, charges just 0.04% a year and the ETF share class held about $66 billion as of October 31, 2026. It returned 13.67% over the last year and 9.21% a year over 10 years at NAV.

People land on GrowthETFs.com searching "$VEU," "$VEU ETF" and "$VEU stock," and our live $VEU fund page shows the price and AUM. But a fund page doesn't answer the real questions: what is $VEU actually, is it a growth fund at all, and does it deserve a spot next to your U.S. funds? That's what this post is for.

Is $VEU a growth ETF?

Straight answer: no. $VEU is a blend fund. It doesn't screen for growth or value. It just buys the whole non-U.S. market, weighted by size, so you get growth names like ASML and Tencent right next to value names like Shell, HSBC and Royal Bank of Canada.

So why is it on a growth site? Because a lot of growth investors use it as the "everything else" piece. If your portfolio is $VOO, $QQQ or $VUG, you own almost nothing outside the U.S. $VEU is the cheapest single ticker way to fill that gap. And as you'll see in the returns section, the last few years have made that gap matter again.

If you specifically want growth stocks outside the U.S., that's a different fund. The iShares $EFG tracks developed market growth stocks only, and the actively managed fund in my post on what $CGGO is picks growth companies anywhere in the world, U.S. included.

This is the part most people miss. The top 10 in $VEU add up to about 14% of the fund. In $MGK, the top 10 were about 68%. That's the biggest difference between $VEU and almost every U.S. growth fund: no single company can sink it.

But look at the top four. TSMC, Samsung, ASML and SK hynix are all semiconductor companies, together about 9.4% of the fund. They make and equip the chips behind the AI build-out. So even a "boring" international index fund now has a real AI hardware tilt at the top.

The 10 year number is the honest one. 9.21% a year turns $10,000 into roughly $24,000. The same money in $VOO at 15.40% comes to about $42,000. For a decade, owning the U.S. was simply the better trade.

But the last year flipped. $VEU beat $VOO over the past 12 months, 13.67% to 12.07%, and its 3 year number of 20.60% a year is a lot closer to the U.S. than people expect. Year to date through November 24, $VEU was up 4.42% at NAV, so it gave back some of those gains in November.

And the since inception number is a warning. 5.34% a year since 2007. It launched right before the financial crisis and went through a long, frustrating stretch. International stocks can lag for a very long time. If you buy $VEU, buy it because you want diversification, not because you expect it to beat the U.S. every year.

The number I'd focus on is the price/earnings ratio. You pay about 15 times earnings for the rest of the world versus about 24 times for the S&P 500. That gap is the whole debate in one line. U.S. bulls say American companies earn that premium. International fans say you're getting the rest of the world on sale. I don't know who's right, and that's exactly why some people own both.

A quick note on cousins. Vanguard also runs $VXUS, the Total International Stock ETF, which adds small cap stocks on top of what $VEU holds. The two overlap heavily. $VEU is the large and mid cap version, and it's what our data and search traffic are about.

I'm a dividend investor, and here's how I see it: $VEU's yield is better than any U.S. growth fund, but it's not an income fund either. Foreign dividends can also have taxes withheld by the home country (you may be able to claim some of it back with the foreign tax credit in a taxable account). Who is $VEU for?

  • It might fit if almost everything you own is U.S. stocks and you want the rest of the world in one cheap fund.
  • It might fit as the international half of a simple two fund setup, next to something like $VOO or $VTI.
  • It might fit if U.S. valuations make you nervous and you want some exposure to cheaper markets without picking countries.
  • It's not for you if you want pure growth stocks. It's a blend fund. Look at $EFG or an active global growth fund instead.
  • It's not for you if you already own $VXUS or a total world fund like $VT. You'd mostly be doubling up.

Here's my honest view. $VEU is one of the best tools in the ETF world for one specific job: owning everything outside the U.S. for almost nothing. 3,799 stocks for 0.04%, no stock above 4.5%. What it isn't is a growth engine. The last decade proved that. Treat it as diversification you hope you need, size it so a long lag doesn't make you sell, and keep your growth bets in the U.S. funds. The risks

  • Currency risk. The stocks trade in yen, pounds, euros and dozens of other currencies. A stronger U.S. dollar lowers your returns, and a weaker dollar helps.
  • Long stretches of lagging. 9.21% a year over 10 years versus 15.40% for $VOO. International can trail for a decade or more.
  • Emerging market risk. China, India, Brazil and others bring political, regulatory and market access risks you don't have in the U.S.
  • Chip concentration at the top. About 9.4% sits in four semiconductor companies, and Taiwan alone is 8.8% of the fund.
  • Foreign tax withholding. Some of your dividend can be withheld by foreign governments before it reaches you.

The takeaway

$VEU is Vanguard's all-world-except-the-U.S. index fund: about 3,800 large and mid size companies from Japan, Taiwan, the UK, Canada and beyond, for 0.04% a year. It's not a growth fund, it's the cheapest way to own everything your U.S. growth funds leave out. It beat $VOO over the last year but trailed badly over 10 years, so own it for balance, not for bragging rights.

FAQ

What is $VEU?

$VEU is the Vanguard FTSE All-World ex-US ETF, an index fund launched March 2, 2007 that tracks the FTSE All-World ex US Index. It holds about 3,800 large and mid cap stocks from developed and emerging markets outside the United States and charges 0.04% a year.

Is $VEU a growth ETF?

No. $VEU is a blend fund (Morningstar category Foreign Large Blend). It owns both growth and value stocks weighted by market cap. Investors often pair it with U.S. growth funds for international diversification. For international growth only, funds like $EFG target developed market growth stocks.

What are $VEU's top holdings?

As of October 31, 2026, per Vanguard: Taiwan Semiconductor (4.41%), Samsung Electronics (1.92%), ASML (1.54%), SK hynix (1.48%), Roche (0.87%), Shell (0.80%), Tencent (0.78%), Novartis (0.76%), HSBC (0.74%) and Royal Bank of Canada (0.72%). The top 10 were about 14% of the fund.

Does $VEU pay a dividend?

Yes, quarterly. The last four payments (December 2025 to September 2026) totaled about $1.96 a share, roughly 2.6% of the November 24, 2026 price. The December payment is usually the largest.
