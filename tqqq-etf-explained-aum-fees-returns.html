$TQQQ, the ProShares UltraPro QQQ, had about $35.5 billion in net assets in late October 2026, per ProShares, which makes it one of the biggest leveraged ETFs in the world. It aims for 3x the daily return of the Nasdaq-100, charges a 0.78% net expense ratio, and returned 51.85% over the year ended September 30, 2026. That's a big number, but notice it's nowhere near 3x the Nasdaq-100's 24.00% over the same year. That gap is the whole story of this fund.

"$TQQQ AUM" is one of the top searches on this site, along with "$TQQQ expense ratio", "$TQQQ fees" and "$TQQQ annual returns". I'm a dividend investor, not a leveraged ETF trader. But I get asked about $TQQQ all the time, and most people buying it don't fully understand what they own. So here's the plain English version with the real numbers.

$TQQQ AUM: why it's so big

About $35 billion is a lot of money for a fund that ProShares itself describes as a daily trading tool. For context, that's bigger than most plain vanilla growth ETFs on our full list of growth ETFs. It currently sits at the top of that list by total return too.

Two things drive it. First, the Nasdaq-100 has had a monster run, and 3x of a monster run is very attractive. Second, a huge amount of $TQQQ volume comes from active traders who move in and out. Big AUM gives you tight spreads and deep liquidity. It does not mean the fund is safe or right for long term holding. Popular and suitable are two different things.

$TQQQ expense ratio and fees

$TQQQ's net expense ratio is 0.78%, with a gross expense ratio of 0.94%, per ProShares. Compare that with $QQQ, which tracks the same index without leverage for 0.18%, per ChartRow's fund data.

That 0.78% isn't the full cost, though. To get 3x exposure, the fund uses swaps and futures, and that leverage has a financing cost baked into the returns. When short term interest rates are higher, holding leverage costs more. You won't see that as a line item. You'll see it as returns that come in under 3x.

Look at 2025. $QQQ did 20.8%. Three times that would be about 62%. $TQQQ did 34.4%. A choppy year eats leveraged returns even when the index finishes well up.

And 2022 is the one everyone should study. $QQQ fell about a third. $TQQQ fell 79.1%. If you put $10,000 in at the start of that year, you had about $2,090 left at the end. To get back to $10,000 from there, you need a gain of almost 380%. That's the math of big losses: the deeper the hole, the harder the climb.

Why $TQQQ doesn't give you 3x over time: volatility decay

This is the part most people miss. $TQQQ resets its leverage every single day. Its goal is 3x today's move, not 3x the move over a month or a year. Over longer periods, compounding works against you whenever the market chops around.

Here's a simple example. Say the Nasdaq-100 goes up 10% one day and down about 9.1% the next. The index is right back where it started:

  • Index: $100 goes to $110, then back to $100. Flat.
  • 3x fund: $100 goes up 30% to $130, then down about 27.3% to roughly $94.50. Down about 5.5%, even though the index went nowhere.

Repeat that kind of back and forth for months, and the leaks add up. In a strong, steady uptrend like 2023, the daily reset actually helps, which is how $TQQQ did 198% when $QQQ did 55%. In a choppy or falling market, it hurts badly. That's why ProShares calls it a short term trading product and why the prospectus warns returns over longer periods can be very different from 3x.

Does $TQQQ pay a dividend?

Technically, it can. ProShares lists the distribution frequency as quarterly, but the payouts are small and irregular, and the Nasdaq-100 itself only yielded about 0.53% as of September 30, 2026. $TQQQ is not an income fund in any way. $TQQQ vs $QQQ: which one should a long term investor own?

For most people, $QQQ or another low cost growth fund. Here's how I see it:

  • $QQQ gives you the same 100 Nasdaq stocks for 0.18%, with no daily reset and no financing cost. You can hold it for 20 years and the math works the way you expect.
  • $TQQQ can beat $QQQ massively in a smooth bull market, and lose most of your money in a bear market. Its long term return depends heavily on the path the market takes, not just where it ends up.

Who $TQQQ is actually for

  • Active traders who want short term, magnified Nasdaq exposure and watch their positions daily.
  • Experienced investors who keep it as a small, sized position and accept it could drop 70% to 80% in a bad year.
  • Not for anyone who needs that money in the next few years, anyone who would panic sell after a 50% drop, or anyone building retirement income.

Personally, I'd rather own boring compounding assets and dividend payers that let me sleep at night. That's how I built my income, and it's why I spend way more time on yield, price decay and total return than on leverage. If you do touch $TQQQ, make it money you can genuinely afford to see cut by three quarters.

The risks

  • Leverage cuts both ways. A 33% drop in the index can mean roughly an 80% drop in $TQQQ, as 2022 showed.
  • Volatility decay. The daily reset can erode returns in sideways or choppy markets even if the index ends flat or up.
  • Concentration. The Nasdaq-100 is dominated by a handful of mega cap tech names. $TQQQ gives you 3x of that concentration.
  • Higher costs. A 0.78% net expense ratio plus the financing cost of leverage, versus 0.18% for $QQQ.
  • Gap risk. A single day crash of more than 33% in the index would, in theory, wipe the fund out. It has never happened to the Nasdaq-100 in one day, but it's the reason leverage needs respect.

My takeaway

$TQQQ is a huge, liquid, very popular fund, about $35.5 billion in assets, and over the last decade it has posted some incredible numbers. But it's a daily 3x trading tool. It gave about 1.5x the Nasdaq-100 over the last 5 years, not 3x, and it lost 79% in a single year. Know exactly what you own before you buy it, size it small if you use it at all, and don't confuse a hot chart with a long term plan.

FAQ

What is $TQQQ's AUM?

ProShares listed about $35.5 billion in net assets for $TQQQ on its fund page in late October 2026. Our GrowthETFs.com data showed $34.42 billion on October 27, 2026. The figure changes daily with price moves and investor flows.

What is $TQQQ's expense ratio?

$TQQQ's net expense ratio is 0.78% and its gross expense ratio is 0.94%, per ProShares, which lists a fee waiver through September 30, 2026. The fund also bears financing costs from the swaps and futures it uses to get leverage.

What are $TQQQ's annual returns?

Per ProShares, $TQQQ's average annual total returns at NAV through September 30, 2026 were 51.85% over 1 year, 65.37% over 3 years, 21.42% over 5 years, 40.81% over 10 years and 43.23% since its February 2010 inception. In calendar 2022 it lost about 79%.

Is $TQQQ a good long term investment?

Does $TQQQ pay dividends?

ProShares lists quarterly distributions for $TQQQ, but payouts are small and irregular. It is a leveraged growth product, not an income fund.
