What Is $MGK? The Vanguard Mega Cap Growth ETF Explained (2026)

$MGK is the Vanguard Mega Cap Growth ETF (now officially the Vanguard Morningstar Mega Cap Growth ETF), an index fund that owns only the very biggest U.S. growth companies: about 52 stocks, led by Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft. It launched December 17, 2007, charges 0.05% a year and held about $35 billion as of September 30, 2026. Over the last 10 years it returned 19.00% a year at NAV, one of the best records of any plain index growth fund, but it's also one of the most concentrated.

People land on GrowthETFs.com searching "$MGK ETF," "$MGK expense ratio," "$MGK top holdings" and "$MGK fact sheet." How the mega cap growth index picks its stocks

This is the part most people miss. "Mega cap growth" isn't a vibe, it's two specific screens, laid out in Morningstar's index methodology.

Step 1: mega cap. Every eligible U.S. stock is ranked by market value. The mega cap group is the biggest companies that together make up the top 70% of the market's total value. There's a buffer (roughly 64% to 76%) so companies near the line don't flip in and out every quarter.

Step 2: growth. Those mega caps are then scored on six growth factors:

  • Expected long-term earnings per share growth
  • Expected short-term earnings per share growth
  • 3 year historical earnings per share growth
  • 3 year historical sales per share growth
  • Current investment to assets ratio
  • Return on assets

The companies that score as growth go in, weighted by market cap. That's how you end up with only about 52 names. Compare that with $VUG, which uses the same family of indexes but across all large caps, and held 135 stocks on the same date.

One 2026 housekeeping note: Morningstar took over CRSP's index business and renamed the indexes, and Vanguard added "Morningstar" to the fund names on July 29, 2026. Vanguard says the objective and management didn't change. Same fund, new name.

Three quarters in tech. That's why people search "mega tech ETF" and find $MGK. It's not marketed as a tech fund, but in practice it behaves a lot like one.

The 10 year number is the headline. 19.00% a year turns $10,000 into roughly $57,000. The same money in $VUG at 17.94% comes to about $52,000. That extra $5,000 is what you got paid for concentrating in the very biggest winners.

But notice $QQQ. The Nasdaq-100 beat $MGK in every period in this table. So if you're picking $MGK because you think it's the purest "big tech" play, know that $QQQ has done that job better lately, at a higher fee.

And the last year has been more mixed. $MGK's 15.51% trailed both $VOOG and $QQQ. Concentration cuts both ways: when a few of the top names lag, there's nowhere to hide. Since launch in late 2007, through the financial crisis, it's done 13.87% a year.

How I'd think about them:

  • $VUG is the default. Cheapest, biggest, a bit more spread out. If you just want "Vanguard growth," start here.
  • $MGK is $VUG with the smaller names cut off. More concentrated, a little more expensive, and it has beaten $VUG over 10 years.
  • $VOOG is the growth half of the S&P 500, which pulls in some names you wouldn't expect. I covered it in what $VOOG is.
  • $QQQ isn't a growth index at all, it's the 100 biggest Nasdaq companies. It has the best recent returns and the highest fee of the four.

The 2026 split and the dividend

If you looked up "$MGK stock" this year and the price looked off, that's the split. Vanguard split $MGK 5 for 1 effective April 21, 2026. Five times the shares at one fifth the price, same value. It traded around $89 to $90 in late October 2026.

$MGK pays a dividend quarterly, and it's tiny. Split-adjusted, the 2026 payments so far were $0.0643 (March), $0.0835 (June) and $0.0765 (September), per StockAnalysis. That works out to a yield of roughly 0.3%, or about $30 a year on $10,000.

I'm a dividend investor, and I'll be straight: this isn't where income comes from. Mega cap growth companies buy back stock and reinvest instead. Who is $MGK for?

  • It might fit if you believe the biggest companies keep winning and you want that bet in one cheap fund.
  • It might fit as a growth satellite next to a broad core like $VOO or a dividend fund like $SCHD.
  • It might fit in a Roth or other tax-advantaged account where you want compounding and don't need cash flow.
  • It's not for you if you already own $VUG, $QQQ or $VOOG. The overlap is huge, and you'd mostly be doubling your Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft.
  • It's not for you if you need income or want real diversification across sectors.

Here's my honest view. $MGK is a great fund that's easy to misunderstand. The name says "growth ETF," but what you're really buying is about ten mega cap tech-heavy companies at a 0.05% fee. The risks

  • Concentration. The top 10 were 67.7% of the fund on September 30, 2026, with Nvidia alone at 13.7%.
  • Sector risk. About 75% in technology by Vanguard's sector definition.
  • Bigger drawdowns. When mega cap tech sells off, there's very little in $MGK to cushion it.
  • Valuation risk. The biggest growth companies tend to trade at high multiples. If expectations reset, prices can fall even if the businesses keep growing.
  • Style rotation. When value or smaller companies lead, $MGK can lag the broad market for years.

The takeaway

$MGK is Vanguard's mega cap growth index fund: about 52 of the largest U.S. growth stocks for 0.05% a year. It returned 19.00% a year over the 10 years through September 2026, ahead of $VUG and $VOOG but behind $QQQ. Two thirds of the fund sits in ten stocks and three quarters in tech. If you can hold through the drops and that concentration is the point, it's one of the cheapest ways to own the market's giants. Just know what's under the hood, and read the prospectus first.

FAQ

What is $MGK?

$MGK is the Vanguard Morningstar Mega Cap Growth ETF (formerly Vanguard Mega Cap Growth ETF), an index fund launched December 17, 2007. It tracks the Morningstar US Mega Cap Growth Index, holds about 52 of the largest U.S. growth companies weighted by market cap, and charges 0.05% a year.

What is the $MGK expense ratio?

0.05% a year per Vanguard, or about $5 a year on $10,000. That's a bit more than $VUG at 0.03% and less than $VOOG at 0.07% and $QQQ at 0.18%.

What are $MGK's top holdings?

As of September 30, 2026, per Vanguard: Nvidia (13.7%), Apple (12.5%), Microsoft (9.5%), Alphabet (8.9%), Meta (5.0%), Broadcom (4.3%), Amazon (4.2%), AMD (3.2%), Tesla (3.1%) and Eli Lilly (3.1%). The top 10 were 67.7% of the fund.

How has $MGK performed?

Per Vanguard, through September 30, 2026 $MGK returned 15.51% over 1 year, 27.41% a year over 3 years, 15.11% a year over 5 years, 19.00% a year over 10 years and 13.87% a year since its 2007 launch, at NAV. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Did $MGK split, and does it pay a dividend?

Yes. $MGK split 5 for 1 effective April 21, 2026. It pays a small quarterly dividend, $0.0765 a share in September 2026 on a split-adjusted basis, for a yield of roughly 0.3%. It is a growth fund, not an income fund.
