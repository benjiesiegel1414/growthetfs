$QGRW is the WisdomTree U.S. Quality Growth Fund, an index ETF that owns the 100 large U.S. companies scoring best on a mix of growth and profitability. It launched in December 2022, charges 0.28% a year and holds about $3.1 billion. Through October 31, 2026 it returned 27.60% a year over three years at NAV, ahead of the S&P 500, but it lagged badly over the last 12 months.

"$QGRW ETF," "$QGRW holdings" and a whole pile of typo versions of the ticker keep bringing people to GrowthETFs.com. So here's the full picture: how the index picks stocks, what the fund owns, how it's done, what it pays, and how it stacks up against the big growth index funds.

How the WisdomTree U.S. Quality Growth Index picks stocks

This is what makes $QGRW different from a plain growth fund, so it's worth knowing the rules. Per WisdomTree's index description:

Step one is the universe. Start with the 500 largest U.S. companies by market cap.

Step two is the score. Every company gets a composite score made of two factors, weighted equally:

  • Growth (50% of the score): half from the median analyst earnings growth forecast, a quarter from trailing 5 year EBITDA growth, and a quarter from trailing 5 year sales growth.
  • Quality (50% of the score): half from 3 year average return on equity and half from 3 year average return on assets.

Step three is selection and weighting. The 100 companies with the highest composite scores make it in, and they're weighted by market cap.

Here's why that matters. A plain growth index mostly asks, "is this stock growing and expensive?" $QGRW also asks, "does this business actually earn high returns on its capital?" Fast growers that burn cash or earn thin returns have a harder time getting in. That's the whole pitch: growth, but from companies that are already very profitable.

This is the part most people miss, though. Because the final weighting is by market cap, the giants still dominate. The quality screen decides who gets in. Size decides who matters.

The 3 year number is great. 27.60% a year is more than 4 points a year ahead of the S&P 500. Since launch, WisdomTree shows a cumulative NAV return of 157.92%, which means $10,000 at inception grew to roughly $25,800 by the end of October.

The last year is a different story. 7.66% over 12 months, versus 12.07% for $VOO. And October alone was rough: down 5.96% at NAV. When the biggest growth names cool off, a fund this concentrated in them feels it right away.

Tracking is reasonable. Over three years the fund trailed its index by about 0.35 points a year (27.60% vs 27.95%), in line with a 0.28% fee plus trading costs.

Who is $QGRW for?

  • It might fit if you want large cap growth but prefer companies with high returns on equity and assets, not just fast revenue growth.
  • It might fit as a satellite next to a cheap core fund, if you believe quality plus growth will keep beating plain growth.
  • It's not a diversifier if you already own $QQQ, $VUG or $SCHG. The top holdings are mostly the same giants.
  • It's not an income fund. About 0.08% distribution yield, paid once a year.

Here's how I think about it. For a growth fund I plan to hold for 10 or 20 years, I want two things: a clear reason it should beat the cheap index, and a fee that doesn't eat that edge. $QGRW has a clear, logical reason. The 0.28% fee is reasonable for a factor strategy, but it's not cheap, and three years of history isn't a lot. The risks

  • Concentration. About 57% in the top 10 and about 21% in Microsoft and Nvidia alone.
  • Tech dependence. 45% information technology and about 63% with communication services.
  • Valuation. A P/E around 29 and price to book around 11 mean expectations are high.
  • Short track record. Launched December 2022, so it has never been through a full bear market.
  • Recent lag. 7.66% over the year to October 31, 2026 versus 12.07% for $VOO.

The takeaway

$QGRW is the WisdomTree U.S. Quality Growth Fund: 100 large U.S. companies picked on an equal mix of growth and profitability, weighted by size, for 0.28% a year. Its three year record of 27.60% a year through October 2026 beat the S&P 500 (and through September it was well ahead of $IWF), but the last 12 months have been weak and it is heavily tied to a few mega cap tech names. It's a thoughtful way to own growth with a quality filter. Just know you're paying more than the plain index funds for that filter. Read the prospectus before you buy.

FAQ

What is $QGRW?

$QGRW is the WisdomTree U.S. Quality Growth Fund. It tracks the WisdomTree U.S. Quality Growth Index, which ranks the 500 largest U.S. companies on an equal mix of growth and quality scores and holds the top 100, weighted by market cap. It launched December 15, 2022 and charges 0.28% a year.

What are $QGRW's top holdings?

As of November 16, 2026: Microsoft (10.64%), Nvidia (10.26%), Amazon (7.80%), Alphabet (6.81%), Meta (6.54%), Eli Lilly (4.23%), Space Exploration Technologies (3.30%), Broadcom (2.99%), Visa (2.42%) and AMD (2.40%). The top 10 were about 57% of the fund.

How has $QGRW performed?

Through October 31, 2026 at NAV, $QGRW returned 10.06% year to date, 7.66% over 1 year and 27.60% a year over 3 years, with 157.92% cumulative since its December 2022 launch. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Does $QGRW pay dividends?

Yes, once a year in December, and it's tiny: $0.05 per share in December 2025 and $0.0704 in December 2024. WisdomTree lists a 0.08% distribution yield and a 0.17% SEC yield as of November 16, 2026.

What is the expense ratio of $QGRW?

0.28% a year, or about $28 on a $10,000 investment. That's higher than plain growth index funds like $IUSG (0.04%) or $VUG (0.03%), because you're paying for the quality and growth screen.
