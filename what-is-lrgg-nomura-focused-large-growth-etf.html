What Is $LRGG? The Nomura Focused Large Growth ETF Explained (2026)

$LRGG is the Nomura Focused Large Growth ETF, an actively managed fund that holds a concentrated portfolio of roughly 15 to 25 large U.S. growth stocks, led by Nvidia, Alphabet and Microsoft. It launched in May 2024 as the Macquarie Focused Large Growth ETF and took the Nomura name after Nomura bought Macquarie's U.S. and European public investments business in late 2025. It charges 0.44%, manages roughly $290 million, and has trailed its Russell 1000 Growth benchmark since launch.

"$LRGG" has quietly become one of the most searched tickers that lands people on this site. My guess is a lot of folks saw an unfamiliar ticker in a brokerage screener, or noticed the name change from Macquarie to Nomura, and wanted to know what it actually is. So here's the full picture, with real numbers and no hype.

Why did $LRGG change from Macquarie to Nomura?

Because the whole business changed hands. Nomura agreed to buy Macquarie Asset Management's U.S. and European public investments business for about $1.8 billion, covering roughly $166 billion in assets. Nomura announced the deal closed on December 1, 2025 (Tokyo time) and rebranded the business as Nomura Asset Management.

SEC records show the trust that holds $LRGG was renamed from Macquarie ETF Trust to Nomura ETF Trust in early December 2025. The ticker stayed $LRGG. Same fund, same managers, new name on the door.

If you own it, nothing about your shares changed. It's the kind of thing that confuses people when they open their brokerage app and see a name they don't recognize.

How $LRGG picks stocks

This is the part most people miss. $LRGG is not an index fund. A team picks every name.

  • At least 80% of the fund goes into large cap growth companies, generally $10 billion in market value or more.
  • The managers run what they call a "two-sided quality analysis" to narrow the market down to a "Franchise Growth Universe" of about 100 companies.
  • From that list, they hold generally 15 to 25 stocks. That's it.
  • They can own foreign companies through ADRs, which is how Taiwan Semiconductor ends up in a U.S. growth fund.
  • Turnover was 20% for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, so this is a buy and hold style, not a trading fund.

Compare that to $VUG or $SCHG, which own hundreds of growth stocks weighted by size. With $LRGG you're paying for a team's conviction picks. That can win big or lag big, and you need to be okay with both.

It's a very different look from the mega cap growth index funds.

The second half of 2026 has been kinder. Growth ETFs sold off roughly 10% from early October into November 5, and $LRGG held up a lot better than most. Does $LRGG pay a dividend?

Barely. It pays once a year in December. The last two were $0.036 per share (ex-date December 23, 2024) and $0.046 per share (ex-date December 31, 2025). That's a yield around 0.15%.

That's normal for a growth fund. These companies reinvest their cash instead of paying it out. I always tell people to be clear about the job each fund has. A fund like this is a compounding tool, not an income tool. Who is $LRGG for?

  • It might fit if you want a concentrated, high conviction large growth portfolio and you believe in this team's quality focused process. Low turnover and names like Visa and ICE give it a different flavor from the pure tech funds.
  • It probably doesn't fit if you just want broad growth exposure at the lowest cost. $VUG, $SCHG and $MGK do that for a few hundredths of a percent.
  • It's not for income. A 0.15% yield won't pay any bills.

Here's how I think about it. When I'm paying 0.44% a year for active management, I want to see a long record of beating the index. $LRGG doesn't have that yet. It's only about two and a half years old, and the record it does have shows a big gap behind its benchmark. That doesn't mean it can't turn around. It means the burden of proof is on the fund, not on you.

The risks

  • Concentration. 15 to 25 stocks, with Nvidia alone over 16%. One or two names can swing the whole fund.
  • Active manager risk. Returns depend on the team's picks, and so far they've trailed the Russell 1000 Growth by a wide margin.
  • Higher cost. 0.44% is more than ten times what the big growth index funds charge. On $100,000, that's $440 a year versus about $30 to $50.
  • Small fund. Around $290 million in assets. Smaller funds can see wider trading spreads and, in rare cases, get closed.
  • Short track record. Launched May 2024, so it hasn't been through a full market cycle.

The takeaway

$LRGG is Nomura's (formerly Macquarie's) actively managed, concentrated large cap growth ETF: about 15 to 25 quality growth stocks, a 0.44% fee and roughly $290 million in assets. It held up well in the fall 2026 growth selloff, but since launch it has lagged its benchmark and the cheap index funds by a lot. If you like the concentrated, quality growth approach, it's worth a look. If you just want growth exposure, the low cost index funds have the better record. Either way, read the prospectus and do your own homework.
