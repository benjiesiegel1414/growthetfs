Why the index matters more than the brand

Picking a small cap growth fund is really picking an index. There are three families here.

Russell 2000 Growth: $IWO

$IWO is the classic. It holds the growth half of the Russell 2000, about 1,120 to 1,130 stocks, and it's spread thin: its top 10 holdings are only about 6.45% of the fund. Health care is the biggest sector at about 29%, per iShares, which means a lot of small biotech companies. Plenty of those don't make money yet.

It's also the most expensive option at 0.24%, and it's been the weakest performer of the group over 1 and 5 years.

S&P SmallCap 600 Growth: $SLYG, $IJT and $VIOG

These three hold the same index, roughly 350 to 375 stocks. The big difference from the Russell version is that S&P's index rules require a company to have positive earnings to be added. That quality screen tends to keep out the cash burning names, and it's a big reason S&P 600 funds have often held up better than the Russell 2000. The top holdings right now include Protagonist Therapeutics, Ryman Hospitality and Viasat, and industrials lead at about 21% in $IJT, per iShares.

Since all three own the same stocks, the fee is the main thing separating them: $VIOG at 0.10%, $SLYG at 0.15%, $IJT at 0.18%. $IJT and $SLYG are much bigger funds, with $7.57B and $4.64B in assets.

Morningstar Small Cap Growth: $VBK and $ISCG

$VBK is the giant of the category at about $22 billion, and the cheapest at 0.05%. StockAnalysis lists its index as the Morningstar US Small Cap Growth Index. It holds about 560 stocks. $ISCG is the iShares take on a similar Morningstar approach at 0.06%, with about 920 holdings and a bigger tilt to tech.

Over the last year, $VBK returned 7.86%, well behind the S&P 600 group. Cheapest doesn't automatically mean best, but over long periods, low fees are one of the few edges you can count on.

Risk: small caps swing harder

Every fund here has a beta above 1, per StockAnalysis: $IWO at 1.22, $VBK at 1.19, $ISCG at 1.14 and the S&P 600 trio at 1.06. iShares puts $IWO's 3 year beta even higher, at 1.47. In plain English, these move more than the market, in both directions. Smaller companies have less cash cushion, more debt sensitivity and get hit harder when the economy slows or rates jump.

Dividends: don't buy these for income

Yields run from 0.42% for $VBK to 0.78% for $IJT and $VIOG. On $100,000, that's $420 to $780 a year. As a dividend investor, I think of small cap growth as a compounding sleeve, not an income source. So which small cap growth ETF is best?

Here's how I'd actually think about it:

  • Lowest cost, set and forget: $VBK. 0.05%, huge, and broadly diversified across about 560 names.
  • Quality tilt: one of the S&P 600 Growth funds. The earnings screen has helped, and they've led over 1, 5 and 10 years. $VIOG is the cheapest of the three; $IJT and $SLYG are bigger.
  • The classic benchmark: $IWO. The most holdings and the most biotech, but also the highest fee and the weakest recent record. I'd want a specific reason to pay 0.24%.

I'd rather own a quality screened small cap fund at a low fee than chase whichever one had the best last 12 months. And I'd size it as a slice, not the core. For most people, a broad S&P 500 or total market fund already owns the companies that matter most. If you're still figuring out where growth funds fit at all, start with what is a growth ETF.

The risks

  • Long underperformance. Small cap growth has trailed the S&P 500 by about 5 points a year over the last decade. That can continue.
  • Volatility. Betas above 1 mean bigger drawdowns when markets fall.
  • Unprofitable companies. Especially in $IWO, a meaningful slice of holdings are early stage biotech and tech names that don't earn money yet.
  • Rate sensitivity. Smaller companies tend to rely more on borrowing, so higher rates can hit them harder.
  • Data dates differ. Fund pages update on different schedules, so small gaps between sources are normal. Check the issuer before you buy.

The takeaway

$VBK is the cheapest and biggest small cap growth ETF. The S&P SmallCap 600 Growth funds, $SLYG, $IJT and $VIOG, have the best recent record thanks in part to an earnings screen, and $VIOG is the cheapest way to own that index. $IWO is the most diversified but costs the most and has lagged. Whatever you pick, know that small cap growth has trailed the S&P 500 for a decade, so keep it a deliberate slice of the portfolio, not the whole thing.

FAQ

What is the best small cap growth ETF?

It depends on what you value. $VBK is the cheapest and largest at 0.05% and about $22 billion. The S&P SmallCap 600 Growth funds ($SLYG, $IJT and $VIOG) had the best 1, 5 and 10 year returns through September 30, 2026. $IWO is the most diversified but costs 0.24%. This is not a recommendation.

$IWO vs $VBK: what's the difference?

$IWO tracks the Russell 2000 Growth Index with about 1,129 holdings and a 0.24% fee. $VBK tracks a Morningstar small cap growth index with about 560 holdings and a 0.05% fee. Over the past year, $VBK returned 7.86% versus 6.00% for $IWO, per StockAnalysis in late October 2026.

What does the iShares Russell 2000 Growth ETF ($IWO) return?

Per iShares, $IWO returned 11.98% over 1 year, 4.65% a year over 5 years and 9.85% a year over 10 years at NAV through September 30, 2026, with dividends reinvested. It has returned 6.49% a year since its July 2000 launch.

Which small cap growth ETF has the lowest expense ratio?

$VBK at 0.05%, followed by $ISCG at 0.06% and $VIOG at 0.10%. $SLYG charges 0.15%, $IJT 0.18% and $IWO 0.24%, per StockAnalysis in late October 2026.

Do small cap growth ETFs pay dividends?

Yes, but very little. Trailing yields ranged from 0.42% for $VBK to 0.78% for $IJT and $VIOG in late October 2026. These funds are built for growth, not income.
