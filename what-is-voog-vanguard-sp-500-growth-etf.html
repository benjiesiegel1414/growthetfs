$VOOG is the Vanguard S&P 500 Growth ETF, an index fund that owns the "growth half" of the S&P 500: about 150 large U.S. companies picked for fast sales growth, earnings strength and price momentum. It launched September 7, 2010, charges 0.07% a year and held about $26.6 billion as of October 31, 2026. Over the last 10 years it returned 17.46% a year at NAV, ahead of the S&P 500, but with bigger drops when growth stocks sell off.

"$VOOG stock" is one of the most searched fund names that lands people on GrowthETFs.com, along with "$VOOG ETF," "$VOOG holdings" and "$VOOG expense ratio." So here's the whole thing in one place: how the index picks its stocks, what you actually own, the returns year by year, the dividend, the 2026 share split, and who I think this fund fits.

How the S&P 500 Growth Index picks its stocks

This is the part most people miss. $VOOG isn't a separate list of "growth stocks" someone hand picks. It's the S&P 500, cut in two.

S&P Dow Jones Indices scores every S&P 500 company on three growth factors, which Vanguard lists on the fund page: sales growth, earnings change relative to price, and momentum. It also scores them on value factors. Companies that look clearly like growth go into the S&P 500 Growth Index. Clear value names go into the S&P 500 Value Index. Companies in the middle get split between the two by weight.

Then the growth side is weighted by market cap, same as the regular S&P 500. Vanguard holds every stock in the index in the same proportions (full replication), so $VOOG tracks it very closely. Over 10 years through October 31, 2026, the fund did 17.46% a year at NAV versus 17.59% for the index. That small gap is basically the fee.

Two things follow from this design:

  • It's not only tech, but it's mostly tech. Because the index is cap weighted, the biggest growth companies dominate. Information technology was 52.9% of the fund on October 31, 2026, versus 37.4% for the full S&P 500 in Vanguard's $VOO data.
  • Some "growth" names will surprise you. Berkshire Hathaway and JPMorgan both show up in the top 15 holdings, and financials are about 9.5% of the fund. That's the factor scoring at work, not a mistake.

The 2026 share split

If you looked up "$VOOG stock" this year and the price looked broken, it isn't. On March 24, 2026, Vanguard announced a 6 for 1 split for $VOOG, effective April 21, 2026. Shares that traded in the $400s now trade around $71.

A split changes nothing about what you own. Six times the shares at one sixth the price. Vanguard notes that historical prices weren't adjusted, which is why its 52 week high still shows $462.81 from April 17, before the split. Returns aren't affected. I covered the splits for $VUG and $MGK too in my $VUG vs $VONG vs $MGK vs $VOOG breakdown.

Who is $VOOG for?

  • It might fit if you have a long time horizon (10+ years) and want to tilt your S&P 500 exposure toward the faster growing companies for almost no cost.
  • It might fit as a growth satellite next to a core fund like $VOO or a dividend fund like $SCHD.
  • It might fit in a tax-advantaged account where you want the compounding and don't need cash flow.
  • It's not for you if a 30% drop in a single year will make you sell. 2022 happened, and something like it will happen again.
  • It's not an income fund. About 0.5% yield, paid quarterly.

Here's how I actually think about a fund like this. I don't need my growth funds to be clever. I need them cheap, simple and something I can hold through a bad year. $VOOG checks all three boxes. What I'd watch is the concentration: with Nvidia and Microsoft at about a quarter of the fund, you're making a bigger bet on a few names than the "500" in the name suggests. The risks

  • Concentration. The top 10 lines were about 59% of the fund on October 31, 2026, with Nvidia alone at 14.20%.
  • Sector risk. About 53% in information technology, versus about 37% for the S&P 500.
  • Bigger drawdowns. -29.47% in 2022 and a beta of 1.18 versus the total U.S. market.
  • Valuation risk. A 25.2x P/E versus 23.7x for the S&P 500. If growth expectations reset, prices can fall even if the businesses keep growing.
  • Style rotation. When value stocks lead, as in 2022, $VOOG can lag the broad market for a long stretch.

The takeaway

$VOOG is the Vanguard S&P 500 Growth ETF: the growth half of the S&P 500, about 150 stocks, held by index for 0.07% a year. Through October 31, 2026 it returned 17.46% a year over 10 years versus 15.40% for $VOO, but it lost 29.47% in 2022 and has been hit hard in November 2026. Over half the fund is tech and Nvidia plus Microsoft are about a quarter of it. If you can hold through the drops and don't need income, it's one of the cheapest, simplest ways to own large cap growth. Just know what's under the hood, and read the prospectus before you buy.

FAQ

What is $VOOG?

$VOOG is the Vanguard S&P 500 Growth ETF, an index fund launched September 7, 2010 that tracks the S&P 500 Growth Index. It holds about 150 large U.S. companies with the strongest growth scores in the S&P 500, weighted by market cap, and charges 0.07% a year.

What is the $VOOG expense ratio?

0.07% a year per Vanguard, or about $7 a year on $10,000. $SPYG tracks the same index for 0.04%, while $IVW charges 0.18%.

What are $VOOG's top holdings?

As of October 31, 2026: Nvidia (14.20%), Microsoft (11.11%), Alphabet (8.50% across both share classes), Apple (7.21%), Broadcom (4.45%), Meta (3.89%), Amazon (3.58%), Eli Lilly (3.25%), Berkshire Hathaway (2.90%) and Micron (2.86%), per StockAnalysis.

How has $VOOG performed?

Per Vanguard, through October 31, 2026 $VOOG returned 8.28% over 1 year, 27.02% a year over 3 years, 11.58% over 5 years and 17.46% over 10 years at NAV, versus 12.07%, 23.37%, 12.02% and 15.40% for $VOO. It lost 29.47% in 2022. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Did $VOOG split, and does it pay dividends?

Yes. $VOOG split 6 for 1 effective April 21, 2026, which cut the share price to about one sixth without changing what you own. It pays small quarterly dividends, most recently $0.1050 per share in September 2026, with a 0.45% SEC yield as of October 31, 2026.
