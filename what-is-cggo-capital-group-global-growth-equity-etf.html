$CGGO is the Capital Group Global Growth Equity ETF, an actively managed fund that buys growth stocks anywhere in the world, with at least 40% of its money outside the U.S. under normal conditions. It launched February 22, 2022, charges 0.47% a year, holds 99 companies and manages about $11.8 billion. Its biggest position isn't Apple or Nvidia. It's Taiwan Semiconductor.

"$CGGO" is one of the searches that keeps landing people on GrowthETFs.com, along with "cggo etf," "cggo ticker" and "Capital Group global growth ETF." So here's the full picture: how the fund is run, what it actually owns, how it has done against the global market, what it costs, and who I think it fits.

How Capital Group runs $CGGO

This is the part most people miss. $CGGO isn't a global growth index fund with an active label. There is no index it tries to copy. Capital Group describes the approach as bottom up: analysts dig into each company, where it does business, its spot in its industry, its products and its supply chain, and the managers build the portfolio one stock at a time.

It's also run the way Capital Group runs its big mutual funds. The portfolio is split among several managers, each handling their own slice. The current list includes Jason Smith, Patrice Collette, Mathews Cherian and Roz Hongsaranagon, all with more than 20 years at the firm. The idea is that several independent sleeves make the fund less dependent on one person's best guess.

Two rules matter more than the marketing:

  • The 40% rule. Under normal conditions at least 40% of net assets go outside the U.S. (or the ACWI's own non-U.S. weight minus 5 points, whichever is lower). That's why this fund looks so different from a U.S. growth fund.
  • Any size, any country. The prospectus lets it buy growth companies of any size anywhere, including emerging markets.

Turnover was 42% over the last year. That means the average holding stays in the fund for a couple of years. It's not a trading fund, but it's not set and forget either.

A few things stand out to me.

It's a chip fund in disguise, partly. Taiwan Semiconductor, Broadcom, ASML and Samsung are four of the top seven, and Western Digital (storage) is in there too. That's a big bet on the hardware behind AI and data centers, and a lot of it sits outside the U.S.

It's underweight Apple. Apple is the largest name in the global index at 4.8% but only 2.0% here. And Nvidia, the stock that dominates most U.S. growth funds, isn't in the top 10 at all.

It isn't very concentrated. The top 10 add up to about 29%. Compare that to a lot of U.S. growth ETFs where the top 3 alone can be 30% or more.

So about 11 points less U.S. than the global index, and a lot more Europe and emerging markets. Almost no Japan. Interesting twist: by where companies actually earn their revenue, Capital Group shows $CGGO at 44.7% U.S. versus 46.0% for the index. In other words, the businesses it owns sell into the U.S. about as much as the market does. The bet is more about where the companies are based than where their customers are.

Let's be straight about it. $CGGO has trailed the plain global index in every period shown, by about 1.8 points a year since launch. That's a real gap for an active fund charging 0.47%.

The absolute numbers are still solid. 20% a year over 3 years is a great run for any fund. But the honest comparison for a global growth fund is the global market, and you could have owned that for a fraction of the fee.

It also swings harder. Capital Group's risk stats show a standard deviation of 15.78%, a beta of 1.22 against the ACWI, an upside capture of 102 and a downside capture of 126. In plain English: it has roughly kept up in good markets and fallen further in bad ones. October 2026 is a good example. The fund dropped 5.76% at NAV while the index fell 1.64%. Through November 9, it had bounced back to a 9.15% YTD return at NAV.

$CGGO vs $CGGR. Same firm, same launch day, same multi manager system. The difference is geography. $CGGR is a U.S. growth fund with Meta, Alphabet and Nvidia near the top. $CGGO puts close to half its money overseas. If you already own a U.S. growth fund, $CGGO adds more diversification than $CGGR would. I covered $CGGR against other active growth funds in $JGRO vs $FBCG vs $CGGR vs $TCHP.

$CGGO vs $EFG. $EFG skips the U.S. entirely and tracks an index of developed market growth stocks. $CGGO can own the U.S., Europe and emerging markets in one fund, with active managers picking.

$CGGO vs $VT. This is the comparison that matters most to me. $VT owns the whole world for 0.06%, and the index it's closest to (the ACWI) has beaten $CGGO since launch. For $CGGO to make sense, you have to believe the managers will add more than about 0.4 points a year over a long stretch. They haven't so far, but three and a half years is short for an active global fund.

Does $CGGO pay a dividend?

A small one. Capital Group lists June and December as its regular distribution months, and the 12 month distribution rate was 1.06% at NAV as of October 31, 2026. The last payment was $0.3978 per share in December 2025, and before that $0.304 in June 2025, per StockAnalysis. Capital Group's own page shows no distributions paid so far in 2026 as of November 9.

That's normal for a growth fund. The yield is a side effect, not the point. I always say give every fund a job. If $CGGO has a job in your portfolio, it's global growth. Who is $CGGO for?

  • It might fit if your portfolio is almost all U.S. stocks and you want one fund that adds international growth companies, with a manager deciding where to go instead of an index.
  • It might fit if you like Capital Group's long term, multi manager style and are happy paying 0.47% for it.
  • It probably doesn't fit if you just want global stock exposure at the lowest cost. $VT does that for 0.06%.
  • It's not an income fund. About 1% yield, paid twice a year at most.

Here's how I think about it. Most U.S. investors are already about 100% U.S. without realizing it, and $CGGO is a reasonable way to fix that while staying in growth companies. But I'd go in with clear eyes. The record so far is behind the global index, and the fund has fallen harder than the market in down months. If you buy it, buy it for the diversification and the long game, not because you expect it to beat everything.

The risks

  • Lagging the index. It has trailed the MSCI ACWI in every period Capital Group reports since launch.
  • Heavier swings. Beta of 1.22 and a 126 downside capture, per Capital Group.
  • Semiconductor concentration. Big positions in Taiwan Semiconductor, Broadcom, ASML and Samsung tie a lot of the fund to the chip cycle.
  • Foreign and currency risk. Nearly half the fund is outside the U.S., so currency moves, local politics and emerging market volatility all matter.
  • Manager risk. Active funds depend on people. Managers change and styles go out of favor.
  • Premium or discount. It traded at a 0.44% premium to NAV on November 9, 2026. The 30 day median spread is a tight 0.03%, but use limit orders.

The takeaway

$CGGO is Capital Group's actively managed global growth ETF: 99 companies, at least 40% outside the U.S., a big tilt toward semiconductors and Europe, and a 0.47% fee. It has delivered good absolute returns, including 20% a year over 3 years, but it has trailed the MSCI ACWI since launch and has fallen harder in down markets. If you want a one fund way to add international growth to a U.S. heavy portfolio and you trust Capital Group's process, it's a legitimate option. If you just want the world, a cheap index fund has done the job better so far. Read the prospectus before you buy.

FAQ

What is $CGGO?

$CGGO is the Capital Group Global Growth Equity ETF, an actively managed fund that invests in growth stocks of any size around the world, with at least 40% of net assets outside the U.S. under normal conditions. It launched February 22, 2022 on NYSE Arca and charges 0.47% a year.

What is the $CGGO expense ratio?

0.47% a year, gross and net, per Capital Group. That's about $47 a year on $10,000, compared with $39 for $CGGR (0.39%) and $6 for $VT (0.06%).

What is the difference between $CGGO and $CGGR?

Both are actively managed Capital Group ETFs launched February 22, 2022. $CGGR invests mainly in U.S. growth stocks and charges 0.39%. $CGGO invests globally, with at least 40% outside the U.S., and charges 0.47%.
