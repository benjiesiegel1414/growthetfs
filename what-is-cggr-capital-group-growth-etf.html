$CGGR is the Capital Group Growth ETF, an actively managed fund where a team of Capital Group portfolio managers picks about 100 growth stocks, mostly large U.S. companies. It launched February 22, 2022, charges 0.39% a year and holds about $25 billion, which makes it one of the biggest active growth ETFs out there. It beat the S&P 500 over three years through October 31, 2026, but it badly lagged over the last 12 months.

"$CGGR ETF," "$CGGR holdings," "$CGGR expense ratio" and even the typo "$CGGQ" keep bringing people to GrowthETFs.com. So here's the whole picture in one place: how the fund is run, what it owns, how it has done, what it pays, and who I think it actually fits.

How $CGGR is managed

This is the big difference between $CGGR and most of the growth funds I've covered on this site. There's no index. Capital Group states the objective very simply: growth of capital.

The fund is run with what Capital Group calls The Capital System. Instead of one star manager, the portfolio is split among several managers who each run their own slice, plus research analysts. The current team listed on the fund page includes Alan Wilson (principal investment officer, 35 years in the industry), Anne-Marie Peterson, Julian Abdey and Andraz Razen, with more names behind the "load more" button.

A few rules worth knowing from the fund page:

  • Mostly U.S., not only U.S. Up to 25% of assets can go outside the United States. As of October 31, 2026 it was 88.0% U.S. stocks, 9.1% non U.S. stocks and 2.8% cash.
  • It can go anywhere in size. It leans to large, fast growing companies, but the managers aren't boxed into a style.
  • It's nondiversified. Legally, it can put a bigger share of its money into fewer companies than a diversified fund.
  • Turnover is moderate. 30% a year per the latest prospectus, so on average a stock stays in the fund about three years.

Here's why that matters. With an index fund, you know exactly what you're getting and you pay almost nothing for it. With $CGGR, you're paying for judgment. The managers decide when to own more Meta and less Apple than the market does. That can help or hurt, and over the last year it has hurt.

What stands out to me:

It's less top heavy than most growth funds. The top 10 add up to about 42% and no single stock is above 6%. Compare that with $QGRW, where Microsoft and Nvidia alone were about 21% (I covered that in what is $QGRW). If you're worried about one or two mega caps running your whole portfolio, this is a real difference.

The bets are visible. $CGGR owns almost three times the market weight in Meta, Tesla and Visa, and less than half the market weight in Nvidia and Apple. That's what active management looks like in practice. When Nvidia and Apple lead the market, a fund like this falls behind. When the managers' picks lead, it pulls ahead.

This is the part most people miss: $CGGR's tech weight is about the same as the S&P 500's. Where it really differs is more communication services (Meta, Alphabet), more health care and consumer discretionary, and about half the market's weight in financials. So it's not a pure tech fund. Valuation is a bit richer than the market. Capital Group shows a P/E of 25.6 for the holdings versus 24.0 for the S&P 500, and price to book of 7.2 versus 5.5, as of October 31, 2026.

The three year number is strong. 24.55% a year versus 23.40% for the S&P 500. In dollars, $10,000 growing at 24.55% a year for three years ends up around $19,300, versus about $18,800 for the index. Capital Group's fund page also gives it a 4 star overall Morningstar rating among 954 Large Growth funds as of October 31, 2026.

The last 12 months were rough. 1.87% versus 12.11% for the market. That's a 10 point gap in one year. Being underweight Nvidia and Apple while owning big slugs of Meta and Tesla is the kind of bet that can cost you a full year, and that's what happened.

Since launch, it's basically tied. 14.58% a year versus 14.43%. After all the ups and downs, the managers have roughly matched the market after fees. Not a disaster, but not the big edge you'd hope for from active management either.

Read that downside capture line twice. In down months, $CGGR has fallen about 1.58 times as much as the S&P 500. In up months it has gained about 1.21 times as much. So the fund beat the market over three years by taking a lot more risk, not by being smoother. Its Sharpe ratio, which measures return per unit of risk, is lower than the plain index. If you hold it, expect bigger swings than the market in both directions.

Who is $CGGR for?

  • It might fit if you believe in active management and want a growth fund that isn't just a mirror of the biggest stocks in the index.
  • It might fit if you want growth exposure that's less concentrated in one or two names. No holding above 6% is a real feature.
  • It might fit as a satellite next to a cheap core fund like $VOO or $VUG, to add some manager conviction.
  • It's not for you if bigger drawdowns than the market will make you sell. A 158% downside capture is not a smooth ride.
  • It's not an income fund. About 0.15% distribution rate, paid twice a year.

Here's how I think about it. For a growth fund I plan to hold for 10 or 20 years, I want a clear reason it should beat a cheap index fund, and a fee that doesn't eat that edge. $CGGR has one of the most experienced active teams in the business and a fee that's low for active. But nearly five years in, it has roughly matched the S&P 500 with more volatility. I'd want to see it prove itself over a full cycle before making it my core. The risks

  • Manager risk. Results depend on the managers' picks. The last 12 months show how far that can lag: 1.87% versus 12.11% for the S&P 500.
  • Higher volatility. 3 year standard deviation of 17.04% versus 12.14% for the index, and a beta of 1.31.
  • Big stock bets. Meta, Tesla and Visa at about three times their market weight, Nvidia and Apple at about half.
  • Nondiversified fund. It's allowed to concentrate more in fewer companies.
  • Foreign exposure. Up to 25% outside the U.S., which adds currency and country risk.
  • Short ETF track record. Launched February 2022, so less than five years of history in ETF form.

The takeaway

$CGGR is the Capital Group Growth ETF: an actively managed, multi manager growth fund of about 100 stocks, mostly large U.S. companies, for 0.39% a year. Through October 31, 2026 it returned 24.55% a year over three years, ahead of the S&P 500, but it trailed by about 10 points over the last year and has basically matched the index since launch while swinging harder. It's one of the cheaper and less concentrated ways to own active growth. Just go in knowing you're paying for manager judgment, and that judgment won't win every year. FAQ

What is $CGGR?

$CGGR is the Capital Group Growth ETF, an actively managed growth fund launched February 22, 2022 on NYSE Arca. A team of Capital Group portfolio managers picks about 100 stocks, mostly large U.S. growth companies, with up to 25% allowed outside the U.S. It charges 0.39% a year and benchmarks itself to the S&P 500.

What is the $CGGR expense ratio?

0.39% a year, gross and net, per Capital Group. That's about $39 a year on $10,000, cheaper than most active growth ETFs but far more than index funds like $VUG (0.03%) or $IUSG (0.04%).

What are $CGGR's top holdings?

As of October 31, 2026: Meta Platforms (5.9%), Alphabet (5.4%), Microsoft (4.9%), Tesla (4.7%), Broadcom (4.2%), Nvidia (4.2%), Visa (3.8%), Eli Lilly (3.1%), Apple (3.0%) and Micron (2.8%), per Capital Group. The top 10 were about 42% of the fund.

How has $CGGR performed?

Per Capital Group, through October 31, 2026 $CGGR returned 1.87% over 1 year, 24.55% a year over 3 years and 14.58% a year since launch at NAV, versus 12.11%, 23.40% and 14.43% for the S&P 500. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Does $CGGR pay dividends?

Yes, twice a year in June and December, and the amounts are small: $0.0274 per share in June 2026 and $0.0424 in December 2025. Capital Group lists a 0.15% 12 month distribution rate and a 0.21% SEC yield as of October 31, 2026.
